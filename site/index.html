What you get

The chart you like, on the account you trade

MetaTrader 5 has your broker and your trades. CheapTrader gives it the kind of chart many traders prefer, and a few tools MetaTrader's own charts don't have.

📈

Charts like TradingView's

Live candlesticks from your MT5 terminal (every tick), all intervals, a watchlist of every symbol your broker offers, price tags, a right-click menu and a layout that is remembered.

✏️

Drawing tools that trade

Long and short position boxes can buy or sell now with their stop and target, leave a limit order at the entry, and size the trade by risk: a % of your balance, an amount, or lots. Trend line, rectangle and channel too.

🐍

Indicators in Python

The usual ones are built in. Write your own as a small function, draw lines, panes and shapes, and run it in a sandbox with a time and memory limit.

⏪

Bar replay

Walk through history bar by bar with a paper account: stop loss and take profit, trade markers, an equity curve and statistics. Nothing reaches your broker.

🔒

Safe by default

Orders from the app are off until you switch them on. It listens on 127.0.0.1 only and refuses requests from other web pages. No telemetry, no sign-up.

🔌

Finds MetaTrader itself

A welcome tour checks that MetaTrader is installed, open and logged in, and connects to it. There is no password, login number or path to type in.

How it works

Up and running in three steps

You do not need Python, Node or an Expert Advisor: it is one Windows program.

  1. Get MetaTrader 5 ready

    Install it from your broker, log in to a trading account (a free demo account is perfect) and leave it open. A minimised window is fine.

  2. Download CheapTrader

    Take the installer or the portable zip from the latest release and run it.

  3. Follow the welcome tour

    It finds MetaTrader, connects to it and shows the drawing tools, indicators, trading and replay in a couple of minutes.

Make it yours

Your own indicators, in a few lines of Python

An indicator is one function that gets the candles as a table and returns lines. Choose whether it is drawn on the price or in a pane of its own.

  • pandas / NumPy inside a sandbox: restricted imports, a time limit and a memory cap
  • Overlays, separate panes, and shapes such as lines, boxes and labels
  • Documentation opens inside the app
def compute(df, params):
    period = int(params.get("period", 14))
    # the candles are a pandas table:
    # time, open, high, low, close, tick_volume
    return {
        "Momentum": df["close"] - df["close"].shift(period)
    }

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. CheapTrader is open source under the MIT licence: no account, no ads, no paid tier. If it helps you, a star on GitHub or a coffee on Ko-fi keeps it going.

Do I need MetaTrader 5?

For real prices and trading, yes: CheapTrader reads from your own MetaTrader 5 terminal (any broker that offers MT5; a free demo account is enough). Without it the program still starts, on made-up prices, so you can try the tools.

Is it safe? Can it trade by itself?

Sending orders from the app is switched off until you turn it on in the MetaTrader menu, and it only does what you click. It runs on your PC and listens on 127.0.0.1 only; other web pages cannot talk to it. There is no telemetry. Trading carries a high risk of losing money, so try everything on a demo account first.

Why does Windows warn me when I start it?

The program is not code-signed yet, which is common for small open-source projects. Choose More info, then Run anyway. You can compare your download with SHA256SUMS.txt on the release page, and the whole source code is on GitHub.

Does it work on Mac or Linux?

Not at the moment: MetaTrader's Python package, which CheapTrader uses to reach the terminal, is Windows-only.

Is it affiliated with TradingView or MetaQuotes?

No. CheapTrader is an independent project. Its look is inspired by TradingView's, and its charts are drawn by TradingView's open-source Lightweight Charts library. MetaTrader 5 is a trademark of MetaQuotes Ltd.
